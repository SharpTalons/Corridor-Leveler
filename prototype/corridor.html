How it works

Two modes

Ride moves the gain all the time, like a hand riding a fader. Use it for voices and anything whose level keeps changing. Hold measures a typical section when you press Analyze, works out one correction, and locks it. Use it for steady material, or to set a consistent starting level across many tracks.

Four ways to measure

  • dBFS: peak level, the loudest instant.
  • VU: classic average meter, where 0 VU equals −18 dBFS.
  • RMS: average power over 300 ms.
  • LUFS: perceived loudness. M is 400 ms, S is 3 s, and I is everything since playback started or Reset was pressed.

When you switch, the target and threshold are converted so they keep roughly the same meaning.

Four controls

  • Target: where you want the level, in the chosen measurement.
  • Response: slow gives smooth, gradual changes that are nearly invisible. Fast reacts quickly to sudden jumps in volume.
  • Threshold: below this it stops adjusting, so silence and noise never trigger a boost.
  • Ceiling: the output never goes past it. Boosts stop short of it, and the limiter catches the rest.

Safety and views

  • Headroom protection: a look-ahead brick-wall limiter at the ceiling catches stray peaks after the gain correction.
  • Expanded view: live spectrum of input against output, plus level and gain history.
