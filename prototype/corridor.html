Corridor Leveler

Working prototype of a Windows VST3 plugin for OBS and DAWs

Set a loudness corridor. When the sound leaves it, Corridor brings it back. While the sound stays inside, Corridor leaves it alone. Pauses and room noise are never boosted. How it decides, 100 times a second

1

Measure three ways

It reads loudness in LUFS (how loud people perceive it), RMS (average energy) and peak (the loudest instant). In Auto, LUFS decides how loud the sound is, a sudden jump in loudness triggers a fast cut, and peak level drives the limiter.

2

Ignore pauses and noise

Anything quieter than Don't boost below is left out of the measurement. Silence and room hum can't pull the level down, so they can't trigger a boost. After a pause it holds the current gain for the hold time. Then it either eases back to 0 dB or keeps holding, depending on your choice.

3

Compare with the corridor

Between Lower and Upper it does nothing. Above Upper, it pulls the sound down to Upper. Below Lower, it lifts the sound up to Lower. It never goes beyond Max boost or Max cut.

4

Pick a speed

In Auto, the further out of range the sound is, the faster it reacts. Boosting is always gentler than cutting, because a sudden boost sounds unnatural and a sudden shout needs to be caught quickly. In Manual, it uses your fixed dB-per-second rates.

5

Catch the peaks

A limiter with 5 ms look-ahead sits at the end and keeps every peak under the ceiling, so the output never clips.

About this prototype. The measuring and leveling logic on this page is the same logic the real plugin will use. Once you're happy with how it behaves, it gets built as an open-source Windows VST3 plugin with a matching window and a normal installer. The plugin will measure in stereo; this page measures one channel to keep it light.
